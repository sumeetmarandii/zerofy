Most small studios keep their notes to themselves. The thinking behind a decision
gets made once in a meeting, written down nowhere, and then rediscovered the hard
way eighteen months later. The Zlog exists to stop that.

It is a blog about the work: website strategy, design, deployment, and the
unglamorous part — keeping a site healthy long after anyone calls it new.

## What this is for

Three things, in priority order.

**1. Writing down the process.** Most of what we know is repeatable, and
repeatable work should not depend on one person's memory. Putting it here means
the reasoning survives the person who had it.

**2. Answering questions before they become emails.** A small business owner
asking whether they need a CMS deserves a real answer. If we have written it
once, we can point at it instead of typing it a fourth time.

**3. Showing the work honestly.** Portfolio pages show results. A blog shows
judgement, including the parts that were corrected later.

## What we will not do here

No growth-hacking listicles, no recycled industry statistics, and no post that
exists only to rank for a keyword. If a piece is here, it is because we learned
it the expensive way and think it is worth the reading time.

## How it is built

The honest answer is that there is almost nothing to it. Each entry is a single
Markdown file with a date in the filename, sitting in one folder. The host
builds it into a dated page and publishes it. No dashboard, no database, no
admin panel to secure, no plugin that breaks when it is three years old.

That sounds unremarkable because it is. It is also the entire reason the writing
will keep happening, because publishing an entry is a text file and a commit
rather than a production.

> If it is not worth writing down, it is not worth doing twice.
