Most small teams do not need a bigger CRM. They need the one they already
pay for to stop quietly losing things.

That is how it usually starts. A follow-up lives in one person's head. A
stock level lives in a shared sheet nobody has opened this month. A customer
who has not been spoken to in six weeks lives in a note that begins
`Re: the thing we discussed`. Each one is survivable on its own. Together
they are a business that has quietly stopped running itself.

RELAY is at [relay.zerofy.me](https://relay.zerofy.me), and it exists to put
people, stock and follow-ups into one clear view — a sharp, lightweight CRM
for teams who want the signal without the software sprawl.

## One desk, three things

The product is an operations desk, and it is built around three things,
numbered because the order is the point.

**01 / Know your people.** Customer and team context kept close, so every
handoff has a home and every conversation leaves a useful trace. This is the
part that answers the question people ask most often and get least help
with: who actually owns this relationship?

**02 / See your stock.** What is moving, what is low, and what needs
attention before it becomes a fire. Not a warehouse system — a short list of
the things you are about to be embarrassed about.

**03 / Move with intent.** Loose ends turned into next steps, sitting in a
clear queue that stops good work from going quiet. A record that does not
tell you what to do next is storage, not a system.

People, stock, follow-ups, one clear desk. That is the whole promise, and
the front page states it that way, which is roughly our editorial standard
for every other claim on the site too.

## The relay standard

The About page carries a section called *The relay standard*. Three
principles, and they are worth repeating here because they were the brief,
not the marketing.

**01 / Make it obvious.** Important information should not hide behind a
maze of menus. Good software lets you find your footing in seconds.

**02 / Respect attention.** Fast load times, thoughtful defaults and a quiet
interface, so whatever energy is left goes to the work that actually
matters.

**03 / Stay in motion.** Every record should help you take the next step. A
CRM earns its keep by creating momentum, not by storing it.

The third one is the hardest to fake, and the first two are the ones most
products get wrong on purpose — a dashboard with eleven charts on it is
easier to sell than a quiet screen that tells you the truth.

## The unglamorous parts we kept

**It loads, and it stays quiet.** Speed is on the principles list for a
reason. The fastest interface in the world is the one that has not made you
wait before it could tell you something.

**A contact page that a human answers.** An email address, a phone number
and a stated response time — usually within one day — because a tool that
asks you to trust it with your customer list has to be reachable when
something goes wrong.

**Straight answers about what it is not.** Relay is compact on purpose, and
that means it will not do everything. A smaller promise you can hold is
worth more than a longer one you cannot.

## Why we built it rather than shipped it

Because we maintain it. RELAY is not something we resell or white-label. It
is a system we needed for our own operation, and then we put a URL on it.
The people who wrote it are the people who get the bug report, and the
unglamorous work — the load time, the defaults, the empty states — actually
gets done, because nobody is going to sell us on the value of any of it.

It is also the second half of the argument this Zlog keeps making. We write
